Three facts to verify before recommending changes

There may already be a tracking system in place.

I would rather understand what works today than recommend replacing it based on a first impression.

Is 321-245-5718 your mail-tracking number?

Your printed piece uses 321-245-5718, while the main website shows 321-259-2000. If that's intentional attribution, it's a good foundation. We should learn whether it links to booked and completed jobs.

Are the website and mailer offers intentionally different?

The mailer lists $39.97/$54.97 oil changes; the current online specials page lists $15 off an oil change. These may be different promotions. Making that difference understandable during booking matters.

Which outcome does your team use to judge the mailer?

Redemptions, appointment counts, average repair order, first-time-customer profitability and repeat visits answer different questions. Which ones are already connected?

Is the repeat delivery deliberate?

Seeing the same creative more than once may be smart frequency management. A simple wave-by-wave report could show whether the repeats are worth the spend.
