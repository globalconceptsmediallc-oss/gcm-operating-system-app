Why I wanted to write

I'm interested in the business behind the advertising.

I own Global Concepts Media, a small marketing agency here in Melbourne, Florida. I've spent 45 years working in sales, management, and business ownership. One thing that experience has taught me is that you can't really help a business grow until you understand how it operates.

I like getting out from behind the desk and spending time with the people who run the business. I want to understand the staff, the customers, the follow-up, and what makes the work profitable before I start recommending new advertising or software.

I'd be interested in hearing how you keep the right kind of schedule, how often new pet owners come back, and whether you even want more first-time inquiries on your busiest days.

I don't know enough about A Picture Perfect Pet to tell you what needs fixing. That's why I wanted to start a conversation instead of sending another sales pitch.

My approach to helping businesses grow

Learn the business first. Then decide what needs attention.

Sometimes that's better marketing. Sometimes it's the website. Sometimes it's what happens in the office after someone calls.

But I don't believe in changing something just because a marketing agency has a service to sell.

Find the leak. Fix the leak. Prove the result.

What working with me is like

I'd rather learn your business than sell you a marketing package.

A more thoughtful first-visit message, rebooking process, or seasonal scheduling approach may be more helpful than another round of advertising.

If we find a worthwhile idea, we can work out what it costs, try something manageable, and decide from the results. If your team is already doing it well, I have no interest in charging you to recreate it.

Start by listening.

Understand the people, the owner or leadership team's priorities, and how new customers become real business.

Look at what matters.

Learn which services, projects or relationships are profitable before talking about more advertising spend.

Only then recommend an improvement.

Work alongside the team, prove the outcome, and don't continue something that doesn't help.
