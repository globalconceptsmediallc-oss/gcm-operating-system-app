Why I wanted to write

I'm interested in the business behind the advertising.

There's no shortage of companies willing to run ads, build websites, and send reports. Those things all have their place. But I've found that you can't really help a business grow until you understand how it works.

I spend time in my clients' offices. I get to know how they handle customers, what their people deal with every day, where new business comes from, and what actually makes those customers profitable.

Sometimes the thing that needs attention is advertising. Other times it's the website, the follow-up after a phone call, or something we wouldn't have spotted without getting to know the operation.

I'm not claiming I know what Heron needs from the outside. I do think it's worth a conversation to see whether there's an opportunity for us to help you build on what you've already created.

— Global Concepts Media

The way I look at marketing

It's not about selling you more marketing.

It's about asking a simple question: Is the money you're already spending helping the business grow the way you want it to?

I'd rather sit down with the owner and understand the numbers, the people, and the challenges before recommending a new campaign or another piece of software.

A few things I noticed about Heron

You've already done a lot of the right things.

Your postcard led me to a dedicated offer page. You've made it easy for someone to learn about lawn care, claim a promotion, and request a quote. That's not the starting point of a business that hasn't thought about marketing.

01 · THE MAILER

You're investing in getting noticed.

It's a clear local campaign with a recognizable offer. I'd be curious what that investment produces after the initial response.

02 · THE WEBSITE

You've connected the mailer to a campaign page.

The campaign has its own destination with quote and coupon options. I'm not suggesting you need another landing page.

03 · THE CUSTOMER

There's value beyond the first service.

Recurring lawn-care relationships can make the economics different from a one-time sale. I'd want to learn how that works for Heron before suggesting a change.

What I can't see from your website is how this campaign performs inside the business. Your team may already measure and optimize all of it. That's why I'd rather ask than make assumptions.
What working with me looks like

First I learn the business. Then we decide if there's something worth doing.

When I work with a business, I want to understand how the office operates and what success actually looks like to the owner. That means talking to the people who answer the phones, follow up with customers, and deliver the service—not just looking at an Analytics screen.

I also want to understand the economics: what it costs to bring in a customer, what that customer is worth over time, and where you're already seeing results.

Only then do we talk about changes. If we identify something promising, we try it, measure the result, and keep it only if it makes sense for the business.

Listen

Learn how the team works.

Visit the office when it's useful, understand customer handling, and learn where the owner sees opportunities.

Look closely

Understand what the numbers really mean.

Advertising spend, new customers, repeat business, and the cost of delivering the service all belong in the same conversation.

Do the work

Improve something that matters.

Make a specific change only when the upside is worth the time and cost. Measure what happened and share the result.

The first conversation I'd like to have

I'd be curious about a few things.

I wouldn't expect you to send me confidential reports before we meet. These are just the kinds of questions I'd ask to understand whether there's a good opportunity.

01 · Acquisition

How are you feeling about the number of new lawn-care customers you're bringing in?

02 · Investment

Do you feel your current advertising budget is doing as much as it could for the business?

03 · Customer value

How do you think about the value of a new customer who stays with Heron for multiple treatments?

04 · Your priorities

What's one part of growing Heron that you'd like to make better this year?

If the answers point to an opportunity, we can look deeper at campaign traffic, acquisition costs, customer value, and what a sensible improvement would be worth. If your team already has it handled, I'll be the first to say so.

A little research I did before reaching out

There was one detail in the postcard journey that caught my eye.

Your campaign page lets homeowners request a quote or claim the $100 promotion. Both paths have a purpose and some overlapping information. I wondered whether bringing them together might make the experience simpler. I don't know whether it would improve Heron's results—that would need to be tested.
