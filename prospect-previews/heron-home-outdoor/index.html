01 / What we actually found

Heron already has the marketing foundations. That's the point.

Heron has a campaign-specific page, online forms, recognizable creative, and an established ongoing lawn-care program. The opportunity isn't to sell you things you already own. It is to test whether one clearer response path helps more existing visitors become service customers.

OBSERVED · MAILER DESTINATION

Campaign-specific page exists.

Your mailer already leads to a dedicated lawn-care offer page. We would preserve that investment, not replace it with another generic landing page.

OBSERVED · PUBLIC FORMS

Two overlapping ways to respond.

A visitor can complete a detailed quote form or request the coupon and optionally ask to be contacted. The public page does not reveal whether Heron already joins these inquiries internally.

OBSERVED · OFFER HISTORY

Two campaign dates, different contexts.

Our printed mailer expired August 31. Heron's public Specials page separately advertises a $100 annual lawn-plan offer through October 31, 2026. Each future mailing should clearly reflect its own approved terms.

Important distinction: We have not seen Heron's internal lead routing or conversion figures. The risk of missed inquiries is a hypothesis to test, not a defect we can claim to have proven.

04 / Exactly what GCM proposes

One next mailing. One controlled comparison. One clear decision.

We would not ask Heron to rebuild its website or buy a new CRM. The first step is to confirm how its existing forms work, then test a revised response experience on a small, approved segment against the current version.

If Heron's two forms already work well together—or a simpler flow doesn't improve qualified customer acquisition—we would say so and stop. That's how a pilot protects budget instead of turning into a long agency project.

What we'd bring to the review

  1. Show the evidence: the current two-path experience, campaign dates and the specific decision we think is worth examining.
  2. Demonstrate the alternative: a working prototype of one combined quote-and-offer journey.
  3. Agree on one real test: use Heron's existing quote and sales system where technically feasible, and check offer eligibility before publishing anything.
  4. Judge business results: compare qualified quote completion, first services booked and new recurring accounts—not clicks alone.
What GCM needs from Heron: a short discussion of their current form routing, source attribution, approved offer terms and a practical way to observe completed customer outcomes.
