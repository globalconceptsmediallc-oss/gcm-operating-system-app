Your first-day daycare postcard makes a strong invitation: experience the facility before committing. The more valuable question comes after redemption: which new guests return for paid daycare, board with you, book grooming, and become long-term Barkingham families?

01 / The campaign we noticed

A welcoming offer that could open a lasting relationship.

The printed offer is memorable because it is tangible, specific, and connected to a real facility experience.

Observed direct-mail promotionFREE DAY
OF DAYCAREBarkingham Palace · Rockledge

Bring this postcard. Try daycare.

The mailer invites first-time customers to redeem one free day of daycare by presenting the postcard. It also introduces the splash pad, indoor playroom, grooming, boarding and training services.

A postcard redemption is a measurable first action. It is not yet proof that a customer will return, purchase additional services or become profitable.

What we can establish: This is the actual printed offer seen by GCM. The postcard has no visible offer expiration date in the supplied photographs; current availability and terms should be confirmed with the business.
The opportunity:

Measure the free-day promotion as a new-customer acquisition program—not merely a count of people who brought in a postcard. Then compare repeat-booking behavior and service value to the real cost of the trial day and direct mail.

Example decision, not an observed result:

Two postcard areas might each produce ten trial days, but one could create six returning daycare families while the other creates two. Measuring second bookings would help guide the next mailing more effectively than looking at redeemed postcards alone.

05 / A proposed GCM package

A focused 90-day new-customer measurement pilot.

Keep the offer and the Barkingham experience at the center. Start by using the records the business already maintains.

Days 1–30 · Discover

Baseline the postcard journey

  • Confirm current promotion terms and actual costs
  • Review any existing redemption and booking-source fields
  • Agree on consent, retention and reporting rules
Days 31–60 · Connect

Measure the second visit

  • Apply a consistent campaign code to future mailers
  • Compare trial completions with first paid bookings
  • Build a simple campaign and service scorecard
Days 61–90 · Improve

Invest in what brings families back

  • Review paid booking frequency and 90-day returns
  • Identify cross-service opportunities without overselling
  • Recommend the next direct-mail, local search or social test

06 / Four questions for a conversation

Find the opportunity before changing anything.

What happens after the free day?

Is the postcard kept or the redemption recorded against the customer’s booking profile?

Can you identify the second visit?

Does the booking system distinguish introductory trials from first paid and repeat daycare visits?

How do customers find other services?

Is there a natural follow-up path when a daycare family later needs grooming, boarding or training?

What would make the next mailing easier to judge?

Would a monthly view of redeemed offers, retained clients and customer contribution change how the budget is allocated?
