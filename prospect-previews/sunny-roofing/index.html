The question worth answering

Which marketing touchpoints create real roofing jobs?

A call, inspection request, and booked installation are not the same outcome. If we can see the whole path, Sunny Roofing can invest in the messages and channels that produce qualified, profitable work.

01 / What Sunny Roofing already communicates

A credible foundation for growth.

On Sunny Roofing’s public website, the business highlights free inspections, online booking, emergency roofing, a 15-year workmanship warranty, certified installers, financing, and residential/commercial services. Those are meaningful reasons for customers to take the next step—if the right customers see them at the right time.

Availability

Important distinction: These are observed public-facing features—not evidence that Sunny Roofing has a marketing problem. The opportunity is to measure how well its existing assets turn interest into work.

02 / Four buying situations

One roofing company. Four very different customer decisions.

A homeowner with a leak is not searching or deciding like a property manager budgeting a roof replacement. A clearer message for each situation may improve both lead quality and close rates.

01 · Urgent

“My roof is leaking.”

Make emergency response visible, preserve mobile click-to-call, and measure how quickly urgent inquiries become confirmed appointments.

02 · Planned

“Is it time to replace my roof?”

Connect inspection messaging, roofing options, warranty reassurance, and financing to qualified replacement estimate requests.

03 · Commercial

“We need a dependable roofing partner.”

Give property managers, HOA contacts, and business owners a distinct path for flat roofs, commercial repairs, and maintenance conversations.

04 · Storm-season

“How should I prepare or recover?”

Build timely education around inspections, preparedness, and legitimate damage response without relying on fear-based claims.

04 / A focused first 90 days

Establish a baseline. Run a useful test. Scale what earns it.

This is a proposed starting structure—not a claim that every item is currently missing.

Days 1–30 · Learn

Find the baseline

  • Review current service and geographic priorities.
  • Audit Google Business Profile and local search opportunities.
  • Trace calls, forms, inspections, estimates, and sales attribution.
  • Agree on qualified-lead criteria and usable reporting.
Days 31–60 · Test

Improve one customer path

  • Choose a priority: repair, replacement, emergency, or commercial.
  • Align landing-page message and calls to action.
  • Test a controlled search or remarketing campaign, if warranted.
  • Check response speed and appointment outcomes.
Days 61–90 · Decide

Put evidence to work

  • Compare cost per qualified appointment by source.
  • Review inspection-to-estimate and estimate-to-job progression.
  • Keep, refine, or stop the test based on actual outcomes.
  • Build the next quarter around proven business priorities.
