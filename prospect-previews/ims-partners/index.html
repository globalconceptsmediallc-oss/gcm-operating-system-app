The opportunity

Not more names. More confidence in who is a fit.

IMS Partners describes a deliberate approach to selecting new clients and prioritizing existing relationships. That makes attendance volume only part of the story. A better decision may be which invitation, evening, and audience produces the most qualified consultations without overwhelming the team.

01 / WHAT IS VISIBLE

A multi-step campaign already exists.

The mailer names a local venue, presents two dates, and offers telephone or online RSVP. These pieces create a path we can map before recommending additional media spend.

02 / WHAT IS UNVERIFIED

The connection to final outcomes.

Do the response records distinguish mailing batches, dates, attendance, scheduled conversations, qualified households, and eventual new relationships? If your current system already does this, there is no reason to rebuild it.

A useful first question: Does the RSVP platform or seminar vendor preserve a source identifier that IMS can reconcile with attendance and subsequent appointments? That answer determines where a simple improvement might help.

One connected measurement path

Follow the result, not just the response.

A prospective measurement framework for the October dinners. These are suggested checkpoints, not actual IMS performance figures.

STAGE 01

Invitation

List segment, date, quantity, creative and campaign/source ID.

STAGE 02

RSVP

Phone versus web response, registration date and selected dinner.

STAGE 03

Attendance

Actual check-in and attendance rate by event date.

STAGE 04

Consultation

Booked and held conversations; appropriate qualification.

STAGE 05

Client fit

Suitable new relationships and cost per qualified outcome.

This can be implemented with aggregate counts or access-controlled CRM reporting. No attendee list needs to be shared with an ad platform, and no individual financial circumstances are needed for campaign analysis.

A low-disruption first look

Work with the campaign you have already built.

With the dinners approaching, the priority is to preserve what can be measured now—not to change the invitation, rebuild the website, or rush another advertising channel into the mix.

01

Before October 27

Identify who controls the RSVP phone and registration platform; confirm what source codes and date selections can be exported, and who has permission to see them.

02

At both dinners

Reconcile registered attendees against actual check-ins, keeping the two evenings comparable without introducing a new guest-facing process.

03

After the events

Review consultations booked, consultations held, appropriate fit and eventual client outcomes in IMS's own approved systems.

04

Before the next mailing

Use the evidence to decide whether to refine audience, follow-up, event dates, RSVP handling or supporting media—and what should simply stay as it is.

Financial-services compliance stays in control.

This proposal concerns marketing operations and measurement. Any changes to seminar claims, investment or tax language, disclosures, advertisements, privacy notices, data sharing or attendee follow-up should go through IMS Partners' existing compliance and supervisory review. No promised investment performance or client results are implied.

What a useful post-event report could answer

Four decisions worth having numbers for.

No dashboards for the sake of dashboards. Just the answers that influence the next mailing decision.

Which invitation performed?

Reconcile cost and RSVP yield by traceable mail source, if the vendor provides it.

Which dinner worked better?

Compare each evening's attendance and consultation rates while recognizing small sample sizes.

Where did interest fall away?

Separate call/web response, no-shows, unbooked consultations and unsuitable inquiries.

What deserves the next dollar?

Relate total campaign cost to the qualified outcomes IMS actually values.
