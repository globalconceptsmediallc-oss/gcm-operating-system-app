Why I wanted to write

I'm interested in the business behind the advertising.

The Bassett piece is about inspiration, craftsmanship and making furniture your own. The Stressless piece gives a different reason to visit: experience a specific chair and decide whether the comfort is right.

Either one might bring someone to the showroom. But with furniture, that first visit may only be the beginning. There can be conversations with designers, choices about finishes and fabrics, quotes, and a purchase later on.

My background is in sales, management and business ownership. I've learned that the most helpful marketing conversations begin with how a business operates—not with a list of services to sell.

I don't know how Indian River Furniture measures these campaigns today. Your team may already track them very well. I'd rather ask than make assumptions from the outside.

— Global Concepts Media

The way I approach the work

It's not about selling you another campaign.

It's about understanding whether the investment you're already making is helping the business achieve what you want.

I'd want to learn how you run the showroom, how the team handles customers, and what actually matters to the owners before recommending changes.

Understand the operation. Find a worthwhile opportunity. Measure the result.
Sometimes the current approach is already the right one.

A few things I noticed

You've already given shoppers reasons to come in.

Two distinct creative approaches suggest two different buying journeys. That's worth a conversation even if the campaigns are already being measured.

01 · BASSETT / BENCHMADE

It's an invitation to imagine something personal.

Customization, dining and upholstery can start a longer conversation. I'd be curious how you learn whether that mailer led to a consultation or later custom order.

02 · STRESSLESS

It's a reason to try the product.

The received piece featured Stressless Wing recliners, a historical savings offer and a visualization app. The printed offer ended August 31, 2026; I'm not suggesting it's still available.

03 · THE SHOWROOM

The real experience happens with your people.

A designer's advice, a comfortable chair, or a thoughtful follow-up could be central to a sale. A mail response alone doesn't tell that whole story.

What I can't see from the outside: showroom conversations, your sales systems, campaign cost, margins or completed orders. None of the ideas on this page should be mistaken for a finding about Indian River Furniture's actual performance.
What working together would look like

First, I'd learn how the showroom works.

I'd want to understand the people and processes behind the sale: how a visitor is welcomed, how design questions are handled, when someone returns, and what helps a quote become an order.

Then I'd ask what your existing advertising reports already tell you. Maybe everything is connected. Maybe one small improvement could make your next mail decision easier. We wouldn't know until we talked.

If there's a worthwhile opportunity, we could start with something focused, measure it fairly, and only expand if the result makes business sense.

Listen

Learn how the team works.

Start with the owner, designers and salespeople—not just traffic charts.

Understand

Follow the whole customer journey.

Consider interest, showroom visits, quotes, and completed purchases where practical.

Improve thoughtfully

Change only what deserves changing.

Test one sensible idea, account for its cost, and look honestly at the outcome.

Questions I'd enjoy discussing

I'm curious how you see these campaigns.

No need to share confidential reports. These are simply the things I'd ask in an introductory conversation.

01 · The shopper

When someone comes in because of a mailer, how does the team usually learn what brought them?

02 · The decision

03 · The campaign

04 · The opportunity

Is there one thing you'd like to understand better before committing to the next advertising campaign?

If the answers reveal an opportunity worth testing, we'll have something concrete to discuss. If not, I'll still value the conversation.
